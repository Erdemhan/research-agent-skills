# Paper Asset Extraction

## Core Rule
> Take the asset from the source that produced the paper. A redrawn copy drifts
> from the published version, and a screenshot carries the page's resolution
> rather than the figure's.

---

## 1. Why not a screenshot

A screenshot of a PDF page is limited by the render resolution and includes
whatever surrounds the figure. Compiling the piece alone gives an image at any
resolution, cropped exactly, and guaranteed identical to what the paper shows —
because it came from the same source.

Equations extract this way too, which a screenshot handles badly.

---

## 2. Procedure

**Step 1. Locate the pieces.** Figures defined in their own files extract
directly. Figures written inline in the manuscript need the block pulled out
between its `\begin{figure}` and `\end{figure}`. The same for tables and for
labelled equations.

**Step 2. Build a wrapper document.** One article that loads the same packages
the manuscript uses, inputs each piece, and puts a `\clearpage` between them so
every piece lands on its own page.

Carry over from the manuscript's preamble: the maths packages, the table
packages, any TikZ libraries, and the shared colour and symbol definitions.
Figures that use project-specific macros will not compile without them.

**Step 3. Drop what gets in the way.**

- Language packages that make characters active. A Turkish `babel` makes `=`
  active and breaks TikZ key-value syntax such as `x={(1.1cm,-0.3cm)}` with a
  `/tikz/x requires a value` error followed by a capacity-exceeded fatal.
- Caption numbering, if the wrapper's numbers would differ from the paper's.
  Suppressing the label leaves the caption text, which is usually what is wanted.
- Citations. Without a bibliography every `\cite` renders as `[?]`. Strip the
  `\cite` calls from the extracted copy when the author names already appear in
  the text, and keep the originals untouched.

**Step 4. Compile, render, trim.** Render each page at 200 dpi or more, then
crop to the non-white bounding box. The trim is what makes the asset usable: a
compiled page is mostly margin.

**Step 5. Record the aspect ratio** of each output. It is what determines the
displayed height once a width is chosen, and it is needed before any layout
decision.

---

## 3. Choosing the source language

Where the study exists in two languages, pick per destination rather than per
convenience:

- A presentation in one language wants that language's figures where they exist.
- Result figures generated by scripts usually exist in one language only.
- Tables typeset in the manuscript can come from either.

Extracting from the thesis rather than the paper is often easier, because theses
tend to keep each figure in its own file while papers inline them.

---

## 4. Checks before use

- The extracted piece matches the published one. Compare against the compiled
  manuscript, not against memory.
- Numbers in an extracted table match the delivered run artifacts.
- Nothing renders as `[?]` or `??`.
- The aspect ratio is recorded and the piece is legible at the size it will be
  shown. A wide, short figure shown at full width is short on the page.

---

## 5. Keeping the extraction reproducible

Keep the wrapper document and the extraction script. When a figure changes in
the manuscript, re-running them is one command, and the derived assets cannot
drift from the paper. Treat the extracted images as build output rather than as
source, and leave them out of version control where the originals are tracked.
